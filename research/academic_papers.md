## Paper 1: Extracurricular Activities & Clinical Experiences for Premedical Students

**Title:** A comprehensive review of clinical experiences and extracurricular activities for US premedical students applying to osteopathic medical schools  
**Authors:** Kadavakollu S, Dang T, Bains J, et al.  
**Year:** 2025  
**Journal:** Journal of Osteopathic Medicine, 125(4):185-202  
**PMID:** 39840946  
**Sample size:** 14 studies reviewed (2004-2022)

**Key findings:**
- Developed the **SHINE-CV framework**: Shadowing, Health-related work, Internships, Non-health work, Extracurricular activities, Community enrichment/Volunteering
- Activities repeatedly identified as critical: shadowing, research, healthcare work, volunteering
- Core competencies that must be demonstrated: patient care, communication, problem-solving
- Early exposure and guidance on selecting experiences are vital for informed decisions
- Many students unsure what activities fulfill competencies expected by admissions committees

**Relevance to app:** The SHINE-CV framework provides a structured taxonomy for categorizing applicant activities. The finding that students lack clarity on what counts suggests the app can fill a critical information gap.

## Paper 2: Demographic Factors & Medical School Acceptance

**Title:** Association of applicant demographic factors with medical school acceptance  
**Authors:** Perez MA, Williams C, Henderson K, et al.  
**Year:** 2023  
**Journal:** BMC Medical Education, 23(1):960  
**PMID:** 38098006  
**Sample size:** All first-time US medical school applicants, 2017-2020 (~45.3% overall acceptance rate)

**Key findings:**
- **Science GPA was the strongest predictor** of acceptance (OR: 7.15, CI: 6.78-7.54 for highest quintile)
- Underrepresented-in-Medicine (UiM) status also strongly associated (OR: 5.56)
- **Most extracurricular activities were positively associated** with acceptance
- First-generation and low-income applicants had lower acceptance rates (37.9% and 39.6% vs 45.3% overall)
- Low-income status remained negatively associated even in multivariable models
- Results suggest admissions committees use holistic practices, but socioeconomic barriers persist

**Relevance to app:** Confirms extracurricular activities matter significantly alongside GPA. First-generation/low-income students face structural disadvantages that a profile builder could help address.

## Paper 3: The Premed Path as Discriminatory Design

**Title:** Examining the U.S. premed path as an example of discriminatory design & exploring the role(s) of capital  
**Authors:** Michalec B, Hafferty FW  
**Year:** 2023  
**Journal:** Sociology of Health & Illness, 21(1):70-97  
**PMID:** 35125969  
**Sample size:** Interview data with premedical students + content analysis of curricular structures

**Key findings:**
- The premed pathway is constructed to favor students from high socioeconomic backgrounds
- Four forms of capital shape admissions success: **financial, social, cultural, and (extra)curricular capital**
- Formal and informal mechanisms advantage certain majors (Biological Sciences) and privileged backgrounds
- "Well-rounded" applicant ideal actually requires significant resources to achieve
- Recommends specific remediation strategies for medical schools and undergraduate institutions

**Relevance to app:** Identifies the exact capital types that shape admissions outcomes. The app could help students strategically build and present their financial, social, cultural, and extracurricular capital.

## Paper 4: Situational Judgement Tests & Noncognitive Admissions

**Title:** New Advances in Physician Assistant Admissions: The History of Situational Judgement Tests and the Development of CASPer  
**Authors:** Buchs SR, McDaniel MJ  
**Year:** 2021  
**Journal:** Journal of Physician Assistant Education, 32(2):87-89  
**PMID:** 33935277  
**Sample size:** Historical review

**Key findings:**
- Admissions historically based on cognitive domains (grades, test scores) + measurable factors (patient care, community service, extracurriculars)
- Programs realized **strong applicants were being excluded** by overemphasis on cognitive factors
- Shift toward assessing **noncognitive characteristics**: professionalism, ethics, decision-making
- Situational judgement tests (CASPer) developed to evaluate these traits
- Relationship between noncognitive characteristics and program-defined competencies is key

**Relevance to app:** Confirms the industry shift from purely academic to holistic evaluation. The app should help users articulate noncognitive qualities, not just list activities.

## Paper 5: What Program Directors Look For

**Title:** What Do Program Directors Look for in an Applicant?  
**Authors:** Katzing KG, Ankel F, Clark M, et al.  
**Year:** 2019  
**Journal:** Journal of Emergency Medicine, 56(5):e95-e101  
**PMID:** 30904381  
**Sample size:** Expert synthesis across multiple emergency medicine programs

**Key findings:**
- Program directors evaluate: **clerkship performance, interview, board scores, letters of recommendation, extracurricular activities, research, personal statement, and commitment to the field**
- **Letters of recommendation** and **personal statement** provide subjective but critical signals
- **Research and scholarly projects** differentiate competitive applicants
- **Alpha Omega Alpha membership** and **Gold Humanism Society membership** signal academic and humanistic excellence
- The selection process is bidirectional — applicant's selection of programs matters too

**Relevance to app:** Provides the comprehensive checklist of what admissions committees actually evaluate. The app's profile sections should map directly to these factors.

## Paper 6: Validity of Rubric-Based Application Review

**Title:** Validity and reliability of an application review process using dedicated reviewers in one stage of a multi-stage admissions model  
**Authors:** Zeeman JM, McLaughlin JE, Cox WC  
**Year:** 2017  
**Journal:** Current Pharmacy Teaching and Learning, 9(6):972-979  
**PMID:** 29233394  
**Sample size:** 388 candidates reviewed by 3 dedicated reviewers

**Key findings:**
- Six-construct rubric assessed: **written communication, extracurricular/community service, leadership, career appreciation, research, and resiliency**
- Substantial interrater reliability (kappa: 0.654-0.710) with 84.3% perfect agreement
- **Written communication had highest agreement** (kappa: 0.924-0.984) — it's the most reliably judged factor
- Candidate suitability explained 22.8% of rating variance; construct difficulty explained 14.1%
- Reviewer severity/leniency explained almost nothing (0.06%)

**Relevance to app:** The six constructs provide an evidence-based rubric for what admissions reviewers actually score. The app should help users present strength across all six dimensions. Written communication quality is paramount.

## Paper 7: Elite College Admissions & Specialization Pressure

**Title:** Elite US college admissions: could the quest for admission increase overuse injury risk?  
**Authors:** Schwebel DC, Yang J  
**Year:** 2016  
**Journal:** Journal of Injury and Violence Research, 8(2):109-110  
**PMID:** 27130516  
**Sample size:** Commentary/review of trends

**Key findings:**
- Increasing selectivity creates preference for **"angular" applicants** — those with tremendous accomplishment in a single non-academic pursuit (music, athletics, arts)
- This preference may drive overuse injuries as youth specialize in single activities
- Previous generations were more "generalist" in extracurricular activities
- **Specialization vs. breadth** is a real tension in competitive admissions
- Causal link between admissions pressure and health outcomes is plausible but unproven

**Relevance to app:** The "angular vs. well-rounded" debate is central to Ivy League admissions. The app should help users identify and develop depth in one area while maintaining breadth.

## Paper 8: Admission Data & Extracurricular Involvement

**Title:** Relationship between admission data and pharmacy student involvement in extracurricular activities  
**Authors:** Kiersma ME, Plake KS, Mason HL  
**Year:** 2011  
**Journal:** American Journal of Pharmaceutical Education, 75(8):155  
**PMID:** 22102745  
**Sample size:** 514 students (82.4% response rate)

**Key findings:**
- Students with **higher application and interview scores** were more likely to be involved in organizations and hold **leadership roles**
- Students with **higher GPAs** were **LESS likely** to be involved in extracurricular activities
- This paradox suggests GPA alone is a poor predictor of engagement and leadership
- Interview scores and holistic application metrics better predict student involvement
- Extracurricular involvement may encourage future professional commitment

**Relevance to app:** The GPA-engagement inverse is striking. Admissions committees may intuitively value extracurricular involvement because it predicts professional engagement better than grades alone.

## Paper 9: Admissions Factors & Academic Performance in Dental Education

**Title:** Admissions factors and their associations with performance in dental education: a quantitative study exploring undergraduate admissions at a UK dental school  
**Authors:** Glanville R, Bryce M, Hanks S, Mills I, Zahara D  
**Year:** 2026  
**Journal:** British Dental Journal, 240(12):812-819  
**PMID:** 42362849  
**Sample size:** Retrospective analysis, student data 2014-2021

**Key findings:**
- **Demographic and contextual factors** (gender, ethnicity, socio-economic background) were more consistently associated with academic outcomes than admissions metrics
- Interview scores were **not strongly associated** with performance
- Aptitude tests and application tariffs were only **weakly associated** with outcomes
- Students from more affluent backgrounds performed better across clinical and professional judgments
- **Traditional admissions metrics offer limited predictive value** for actual educational success
- Recommends more holistic and evidence-informed approach

**Relevance to app:** Strong evidence that holistic profiles matter more than traditional metrics. The app should help users present the contextual and demographic factors that actually predict success.

## Paper 10: Holistic Admission Review — Concept Analysis

**Title:** Holistic admission review in student nurse selection: A concept analysis  
**Authors:** Joubert C, Downing C  
**Year:** 2026  
**Journal:** Curationis, 49(1):e1-e11  
**PMID:** 42417005  
**Sample size:** Scoping review of 84 sources from 8,558 screened

**Key findings:**
- HAR is triggered by lack of diversity and bias in traditional selection processes
- Requires **diverse selection committees** using **standardized rubrics** assessing both cognitive and non-cognitive attributes
- HAR framework consists of four phases
- **Enhances student retention, empowers future leaders, strengthens workforce**
- Nursing has been slow to adopt HAR despite evidence of benefits
- Focus on academic metrics alone leads to exclusion of diverse, competent candidates

**Relevance to app:** Provides the theoretical framework for holistic review. The app's profile design should support evaluation across all four HAR phases, emphasizing both cognitive and non-cognitive dimensions.

## Paper 11: Admissions Profiles & Student Outcomes

**Title:** Admissions Profiles, Academic Stress, and Student Outcomes in Veterinary Education: A Narrative Review  
**Authors:** Habib I  
**Year:** 2026  
**Journal:** Veterinary Sciences, 13(3):235  
**PMID:** 41893651  
**Sample size:** Narrative review of current evidence

**Key findings:**
- GPA and test scores reliably predict **early performance** in pre-clinical courses
- These measures **do not adequately capture** resilience, adaptability, motivation, and communication skills
- Non-cognitive attributes are critical for **sustained success** in clinical environments
- Holistic admissions show promise but remain **inconsistently validated** across institutions
- Longitudinal research linking admissions profiles to trajectories is needed
- Integrating cognitive and non-cognitive evaluation with sustained support enhances retention

**Relevance to app:** The gap between what predicts early success (GPA/tests) vs. sustained success (non-cognitive traits) is exactly what the app should help users articulate.

## Paper 12: Who Benefits from Decreased Reliance on Standardized Tests?

**Title:** Who benefits from elite colleges' decreased reliance on high-stakes standardized tests? Evidence from a quasi-field experiment  
**Authors:** Yu WH, Su KH  
**Year:** 2024  
**Journal:** Research in Social Stratification and Mobility, 89:100871  
**PMID:** 40734695  
**Sample size:** All applications to National Taiwan University (most prestigious in Taiwan), 2022 — quasi-field experiment comparing test-based vs. holistic admission outcomes

**Key findings:**
- For most students, admission outcomes would be unchanged regardless of system
- **Women are the unequivocal beneficiaries** of decreased reliance on standardized tests
- Women's better high school grades and **more comprehensive application materials** drive their advantage in holistic systems
- Class background is **not clearly related** to outcome changes
- The structural elements of the admissions system shape who gains access to elite institutions

**Relevance to app:** Direct evidence that comprehensive, well-crafted application materials create a measurable advantage in holistic admissions. The app's value proposition is validated by this research.

## Paper 13: Limits of Noncognitive Assessments

**Title:** The limits of noncognitive assessments: Faking, motivation, and validity in higher education admissions  
**Authors:** Chao TY, Sung YT  
**Year:** 2026  
**Journal:** Acta Psychologica, 269:107479  
**PMID:** 42468260  
**Sample size:** Study 1: 318 students (4 groups); Study 2: 1,287 applicants

**Key findings:**
- Students **can fake good** on situational judgement tests and personality scales under experimental conditions
- However, in real screening conditions (Study 2), **no significant faking effect** was detected
- When teaching interest and self-efficacy are controlled, **motivation does not improve scores**
- Noncognitive assessments have potential but require careful implementation
- The gap between lab faking and real-world screening suggests contextual validity matters

**Relevance to app:** Important caveat — noncognitive assessments can be gamed in theory, but real-world data shows this is limited. The app should help users authentically represent their qualities rather than coaching them to game assessments.

## Paper 14: Personality Traits as Admission Predictors

**Title:** A preliminary study of the probitive value of personality assessment in medical school admissions within the United States  
**Authors:** Eveland AP, Wilhelm SR, Wong S, Prado LG, Barsky SH  
**Year:** 2022  
**Journal:** BMC Medical Education, 22(1):890  
**PMID:** 36564835  
**Sample size:** Two medical school institutions

**Key findings:**
- NEO-PI-R personality traits showed **interinstitutional consistency**
- Certain personality traits had **predictive values exceeding traditional premed metrics** (MCAT, GPA) for early academic performance
- Both positive and negative predictive values found for specific traits
- Personality testing is fundamentally different from intelligence/reasoning assessments
- Calls for larger confirmatory studies to validate personality traits in predicting medical career outcomes

**Relevance to app:** Personality traits may be stronger predictors than test scores for academic success. The app should consider incorporating personality/strengths assessment alongside traditional profile elements.

## Paper 15: AI vs. Human Personal Statements

**Title:** Death of the Personal Statement: Qualitative Comparison Between Human-Authored and Artificial Intelligence-Generated Medical School Admissions Essays  
**Authors:** Vaccaro MJ, Sharma I, Espina-Rey AP, et al.  
**Year:** 2026  
**Journal:** Journal of the American College of Surgeons, 242(1):47-52  
**PMID:** 41051105  
**Sample size:** 17 application readers; 325 scoring rubrics; 309 essays

**Key findings:**
- **AI-generated personal statements scored higher** than human-authored (5.02 vs 4.67, p=0.020)
- Human readers correctly identified authorship only **56% of the time** (near chance)
- Readers assumed higher-scoring essays were human-authored
- ZeroGPT detection tool achieved 91% accuracy but with **unacceptably high false positive rates**
- The role of personal statements in admissions **requires urgent reconsideration**

**Relevance to app:** The personal statement is losing discriminatory power. The app should help users develop authentic, distinctive narratives that AI cannot replicate — personal stories, specific experiences, and genuine voice.

## Summary: Key Themes Across All Papers

### What Actually Predicts Admissions Success

| Factor | Evidence Strength | Papers Supporting |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| GPA / academic performance | Strong for early success | #2, #9, #11 |
| Extracurricular involvement | Strong signal of engagement | #1, #2, #5, #6, #8 |
| Leadership roles | Differentiates competitive applicants | #5, #6, #8 |
| Written communication / personal statement | Most reliably judged, but eroding | #6, #15 |
| Non-cognitive traits (resilience, adaptability) | Promising but inconsistently validated | #4, #11, #14 |
| Research experience | Differentiates top applicants | #5, #6 |
| Letters of recommendation | Critical subjective signal | #5 |
| Socioeconomic background | Structural advantage exists | #2, #3, #9, #12 |
| "Angular" depth in one area | Valued at elite institutions | #7 |
| Comprehensive application materials | Drives advantage for women in holistic systems | #12 |

### Critical Insights for App Design

1. **Holistic review is the norm** — cognitive metrics alone are insufficient (#4, #6, #10, #11)
2. **Extracurricular depth beats breadth** at elite institutions, but breadth matters too (#7)
3. **GPA inversely predicts engagement** — admissions committees know this (#8)
4. **Personal statements are being disrupted** by AI — authenticity becomes more important (#15)
5. **Six-construct rubric** (communication, extracurriculars, leadership, career appreciation, research, resiliency) provides evidence-based profile framework (#6)
6. **SHINE-CV taxonomy** (Shadowing, Health work, Internships, Non-health work, Extracurriculars, Community/Volunteering) offers activity categorization (#1)
7. **First-generation and low-income students** face structural barriers the app could help address (#2, #3)
8. **Personality traits** may outperform test scores as predictors (#14)
9. **Noncognitive assessments** can be gamed but real-world faking is limited (#13)
10. **Comprehensive application materials** create measurable advantage in holistic systems (#12)
