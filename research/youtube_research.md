## Video 1: "How to Get Into MIT"
- **Channel**: MIT Admissions / College admissions counselor
- **Video ID**: HBBM2gK2aJI
- **URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HBBM2gK2aJI
- **Topic**: MIT Maker Portfolio

### Key Advice
- MIT (and other colleges) accept a **maker portfolio** alongside traditional art/music portfolios
- Maker portfolio showcases **robots, engineering projects, things built in garage/lab**
- An **expert professor of engineering** evaluates the portfolio and gives it a reading
- The professor's assessment goes to the admissions committee
- **Risk**: If you spend 40 hours/week on a project and the professor says it's "not that great," it could hurt your application
- Submit only your **best project** that showcases highest technical skill

### Specific Activities Mentioned
- Building robots
- Engineering projects in a lab or garage
- Coding apps (10 hours/week)
- Building things independently
- Any project that demonstrates technical skill

### What Made Applicants Stand Out
- Showcasing a specific **problem you're trying to solve**
- Genuine passion + technical skill demonstrated through tangible work
- Self-directed projects (not limited to school clubs/activities)
- Portfolio quality reviewed by domain expert = credibility signal

### Key Quotes
> "You're not limited to only official clubs and activities at school. You're more than welcome to include things like building in your garage, coding apps for 10 hours a week."

> "Find what it is that you love and pursue it, and then it's so much easier to fill it in on the application later on."

## Video 2: "here's what IVY LEAGUE admissions REALLY look for"
- **Channel**: College admissions / Ivy League guide channel
- **Video ID**: cTLp2x6LaeE
- **URL**: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cTLp2x6LaeE
- **Topic**: Comprehensive breakdown of what each Ivy League school looks for

### Key Advice Per School

#### Harvard
- Looking for the **"undeniable"** — students doing something real with weight
- Something **personal to their story**, not just checking off boxes
- Finding: nonprofit founder, slam poet, quantum physicist all walk together
- They want students **building something**, not just accumulating activities

#### Yale
- Don't need a Nobel Prize
- **Essays must have soul** — insight, maturity, thoughtfulness
- Looking for **how you think**, not just what you've done
- "If your teachers say things like 'quiet but deep thinker,' Yale will be listening"

#### Princeton
- Looking for **scholars, not resume builders, not clout chasers**
- Pure **academic curiosity**, especially in writing and research
- Obsessed with **independent work** (senior thesis required for all non-CS majors)
- If you write 30-page papers and start arguments in English class for fun → Princeton

#### Columbia
- **Intellectually restless** students
- People who like **dense ideas**, thrive in fast-paced environments
- Can handle **academic pressure without panicking**
- Reads philosophy for fun, gets invested in classroom debates

#### Brown
- **Self-driven** students who make their own path
- **Authenticity** — not chasing clout but chasing ideas
- The **open curriculum** means they want students who are self-directed
- "If you've ever written a 2,000-word blog post on a hyper-niche topic no one else cared about" → Brown loves that

#### Dartmouth
- **Genuinely collaborative** students — the glue in a group
- Adventurous, outdoorsy, intellectually curious, **a little offbeat**
- Tight-knit community values

### Universal Themes Across Ivies
- **Depth over breadth** — doing something meaningful vs. checking boxes
- **Authenticity** and genuine passion
- **Intellectual curiosity** as core trait
- Each school has a distinct personality; match your application to fit
- **Story/personal narrative** matters more than raw achievements

## Synthesis: Key Patterns for College Admissions Profiles

### What Successful Applicants Have in Common

1. **Depth Over Breadth** — 3-5 deeply developed activities beat 10 shallow ones
2. **Genuine Passion** — pursuing interests for their own sake, not just for applications
3. **Impact Narrative** — clear story of how they made a difference
4. **Self-Directed Projects** — not limited to school-provided activities
5. **Personal Story** — authentic narrative connecting everything together
6. **Intellectual Curiosity** — genuine love of learning, not just GPA chasing

### MIT-Specific Maker Portfolio Insights

- **What to include**: Robots, engineering projects, coding projects, garage builds
- **Quality gate**: Expert professor evaluates — weak work can HURT application
- **Strategy**: Submit your absolute best single project
- **Proof of skill**: Must demonstrate real technical ability
- **Problem-solving focus**: Show what problem you're trying to solve
- **Independent work**: 10+ hours/week on a passion project is impressive

### Ivy League School Profiles (Activities That Match)

| School | Ideal Applicant Profile |
|--------|------------------------|
| Harvard | "The Undeniable" — doing something real and personal with weight |
| Yale | Deep thinker, soulful essays, insight + maturity |
| Princeton | Pure scholar, academic curiosity, loves independent research |
| Columbia | Intellectually restless, thrives under pressure, reads philosophy |
| Brown | Self-driven, authentic, quirky, chases ideas not clout |
| Dartmouth | Collaborative glue, adventurous, outdoorsy, intellectually curious |
| Cornell | (Not covered in detail in transcripts) |
| Penn | (Not covered in detail in transcripts) |

### Extracurricular Activity Categories That Stand Out

1. **Founding something** — nonprofit, club, startup, organization
2. **Research** — lab work, independent study, publications
3. **Community service** — genuine impact, not performative
4. **Maker/engineering projects** — building tangible things
5. **Coding/tech projects** — apps, websites, open-source contributions
6. **Passion projects** — niche interests pursued deeply
7. **Leadership roles** — but showing real impact, not just titles
8. **Athletics** — especially for schools like Harvard with extensive programs

### Essay Themes That Work

1. **Bold openings** — hook readers immediately with surprising statements
2. **Vulnerability** — being honest about struggles, identity, privilege
3. **Cross-disciplinary thinking** — connecting disparate interests
4. **Cultural narrative** — immigrant experience, cultural adjustment
5. **Self-awareness** — understanding your position and wanting to make a difference
6. **Authentic voice** — personal, not formulaic

### Application Strategy Takeaways

1. **8 minutes**: That's how long admissions officers spend per application
2. **Top 3-5 activities** carry the most weight — make them count
3. **10 activity spots** — use all if balanced; quality matters most
4. **Portfolio optional but risky** — only submit if expert-level quality
5. **Match school personality** — different Ivies want different types
6. **Passion > prestige** — genuine interest beats résumé padding every time
